The 4-Step Meeting Formula react · dig · curiosity · ask
1. React

Make them feel heard

"Got it." · "That makes sense." · "I hear that a lot."

2. Dig

One easy follow-up

"How has that been working?" · "What makes that frustrating?" · "IT or users?"

3. Curiosity

Share one pattern

"A lot of teams already have a tool. The gaps are usually manual work, mobile security, or support."

4. Ask

Sell the next step

"I don't know if MaaS360 is relevant yet. Would 10-15 minutes be unreasonable?"

Pro Tips & Strategy
•
Your goal is not to pitch MaaS360

Get them talking, learn one useful thing, create a little curiosity, and earn 10-15 minutes on the calendar.

•
One question, one insight, one ask

Ask a simple question, react, share a short pattern you're seeing, then ask for a quick meeting. Don't stack five discovery questions.

•
Sound like you're calling a coworker

Short sentences. Normal words. Calm pace. If a line is hard to say out loud, rewrite it until it sounds like you.

Curious method

Best for: most live connects

Start with a low-pressure question that gets them talking before you explain anything.

"When it comes to company devices, what's usually the biggest headache for your team?"

Pattern method

Best for: creating a reason to meet

After they answer, mention a common pattern that may or may not apply.

"A lot of teams already have a tool, but still hit gaps around a clearer view, mobile security, or manual support work."

Referral method

Best for: unclear title

Ask for direction instead of forcing the pitch on the wrong person.

"You may not be the right person, but who usually owns device management on your side?"

Cadence · Attempt 1Call, leave the short voicemail, send the follow-up email.
Attempt 2Call 2-3 days later. Don't repeat the same voicemail.
Attempt 3Call ~1 week later. If no answer, move to nurture or wait for a trigger.
Reason to callHiring, growth, new locations, remote work, compliance, or device-heavy teams.
Likely personaIT Director, Security, Ops, Infrastructure, EUC, Help Desk, or CIO.
