Pro tips and strategy

🔥
The power move

Say "I'm the MaaS360 [AE/POC] for [Company Name]" — it sounds like you were assigned to them specifically, not like you're blasting a list. They feel like the call was coming regardless. More authority than "I'm calling to introduce myself."

💪
Fight for every second

Be positive. Your default response: "I'm glad you brought that up." Keep fighting — even if they're rude, stay professional: "Thank you for sharing that, that's exactly why I wanted to talk to you today." Never give up easily.

Glaze method

Best for: Natural yappers who can build rapport quickly

Build warmth first. Reference something real about them or their company. Compliment genuinely. Let them talk before pivoting to the ask.

"I saw you just expanded to three new locations — that's impressive growth."

Direct method

Best for: Getting straight to business

Skip the small talk. Go straight to the ask. Respect their time by being efficient.

"Are you open to a 10-minute call to see if we're a fit?"

Permission method

Best for: Guarded prospects and busy leaders

Name the interruption first, ask for 20 seconds, then let them choose. This feels more human than forcing a pitch.

"You weren't expecting my call. Can I take 20 seconds to tell you why I reached out, and you can tell me if it's off base?"

Delivery reminders

Say "for [Company Name]" with ownership, not apology
Pause after your intro — let it settle
Always end with two specific times
Sound like you expected them to pick up
Don't rush — slower reads as more confident
No "I was just calling to..."
Don't pitch the product on the cold call
Ask, then go quiet — let them answer

Mindset

You own this territory — act like it
Selling 10 minutes, not MaaS360
Curious beats pushy every time
Less words, more confidence
One smooth pass, then listen

Discovery flow

✅
Think branch, not question bank

Ask one primary question. Based on their answer, use one follow-up. Then route to the meeting. If they say yes to a meeting, do not keep qualifying them on the cold call.

Primary question
"Out of curiosity, how are you handling device management and security today?"

If they use Intune, Workspace ONE, Jamf, etc.

"Makes sense. What made you choose that platform originally?"

Follow-up: "If you could improve one part of the current setup, what would it be?"

If they say it works fine

"Good to hear. Most teams I talk to already have something in place."

Follow-up: "Any areas where it still creates extra work for IT?"

If they mention a pain

"That makes sense. How are you handling that today?"

Transition: "That is probably easier to show than explain over the phone."
Land here

"Based on that, it is probably worth a quick look. Does Tuesday at 2 work, or is Wednesday morning better?"

"When you think about endpoint management, what is the bigger priority right now: reducing risk, reducing IT workload, or supporting growth?"

"Is that being driven by compliance, cyber insurance, or overall security posture?"

If efficiency

"Where is the team spending the most manual effort today?"

Then: tie it to admin workload and consolidation.

MaaS360 value snapshot

Use this only when someone asks, "What does MaaS360 actually do?" Pick one benefit, connect it to their role, then return to the meeting ask.

Unified endpoint management

Manage and secure phones, tablets, laptops, and other endpoints from one place instead of juggling separate tools.

Mobile threat defense

Help protect users, devices, apps, and data from threats like malware, phishing, and risky networks.

Less manual IT work

Support enrollment, configuration, app access, lockouts, and device support with fewer repetitive tasks.

BYOD boundaries

Separate corporate data from personal data so employees keep privacy while IT protects the business side.

Compliance visibility

Apply policies, monitor posture, and support audit readiness across employees, devices, and locations.

IBM enterprise backing

Enterprise-grade platform, security credibility, and support for organizations that need scale and trust.

Do not read this whole section on a cold call. One value point is enough. If they ask for more, use that interest to book the meeting.

Always close with this

After any objection, land here

"Does Tuesday at 2 work, or is Wednesday morning better for you?"

If they won't commit to either time:

"No worries — what's your calendar usually look like, mornings or afternoons?" Get a general window, then follow up by email to lock the exact time. Some people will book a meeting but won't pick from two fixed options on the spot — don't let the call end without at least a window.
If they ask what MaaS360 does:

"It's IBM's platform for managing and securing mobile devices and endpoints — takes a lot of the manual IT work off your plate while keeping everything locked down. Easier to show you with a real example than explain it over the phone — that's why I want the 10 minutes."

Voicemail script

"Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with IBM — I'm the MaaS360 [AE/POC] for [Company Name]. We help companies manage and secure devices from one place without adding more work for IT, and I wanted to see if it made sense for us to connect. I'll send you an email, but if you want to chat sooner, my number is [Your Number]."
Keep it under 20 seconds. Sound confident, not desperate. Always mention you'll follow up with email.

Gatekeeper script

"Hey, this is [Your Name] with IBM — I'm trying to reach [Decision Maker Name]. Are they available?"
Sound like you already have a relationship. Don't over-explain. If they ask what it's about: "It's regarding their mobile device security setup — they'll know what it's about."
If they push back: "I understand — I'm the MaaS360 [AE/POC] for [Company Name] and I just wanted to introduce myself. Would it be better if I sent an email first?"

Cadence — if no answer

Attempt 1: Call. No answer → leave voicemail, then send the follow-up email same day.
Attempt 2 (2-3 days later): Call again. No voicemail this time — you already left one. If no answer, a short LinkedIn connection request referencing the voicemail is optional here.
Attempt 3 (about a week later): Call one more time. If still no answer, this is the point to leave it — don't leave a second voicemail; it reads as pushy rather than persistent.
After attempt 3: Move to a longer-cycle nurture — quarterly check-in, or triggered by news (funding, leadership change, security incident) rather than a fixed calendar date.

Quick company research (30 seconds)

LinkedIn: Check their profile — tenure, recent posts, mutual connections
Company news: Search "[Company Name] news" — recent funding, expansion, leadership changes
Company website: About page, recent blog posts, press releases
Industry: Know 1-2 similar companies you've worked with in their space
Size/scale: Employee count, number of locations (helps tailor your pitch)

Tonality and delivery

Pace: Slower than you think. Rushing reads as nervous. Confident people don't rush.
Pauses: After your intro, pause 1-2 seconds. Let it land. Don't fill the silence.
Energy: Match their energy. If they're high-energy, match it. If they're calm, stay calm.
Smile: They can hear it. Smile while you talk — it changes your tone.
Confidence: Sound like you expected them to pick up. You're not interrupting — you're offering value.

Red flags vs. green flags

Green flags (they're interested)

They ask questions about MaaS360
They mention current pain points
They offer specific times to meet
They ask about pricing/timeline
They mention upcoming projects/changes

Red flags (just being polite)

Vague responses: "Maybe later"
"Send me some info" with no follow-up
They don't ask any questions
They're distracted/multitasking
"We're all set" without details
