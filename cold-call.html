Consultation Opener verbatim · say this

Confident "complimentary review" invite. Frames the call as an IBM review you're setting aside time for, not a generic pitch. It's about improving their company, and it asks for the meeting early. Say the beats, pause on "your team stood out," then ask.

confident open → the invite → the ask — ~20 seconds, one breath per beat

When They Say No tap a card

"No" isn't one thing — there are three, and each gets handled differently. Your only real loss is hanging up with nothing. Walk away with a callback, a reason, or permission to follow up.

convert every no → a time, a reason, or a next touch
1 · "No" to the 30 seconds (permission no)▾

"Totally fair, I caught you cold. Worst case — when's a better time to try you, later today or tomorrow morning?"

don't push into the pitch — just book the callback and go
2 · "Not interested" (early reflex)▾

"Yeah, that's fair — I kind of expected that since I called you out of nowhere. Can I ask, is it that you've already got this handled, or just that it's not a priority right now?"

their answer tells you: real no vs. brush-off
3 · "No" to the meeting (most important)▾

"No problem at all. Before I let you go — what would need to change for this to be worth a look down the road?"

then pin it: "Mind if I check back in a quarter? I'll send my info now."

Pro Tips & Strategy
•
Your goal is not to pitch MaaS360

Get them talking, learn one useful thing, create a little curiosity, and earn 10-15 minutes on the calendar.

•
One question, one insight, one ask

Ask a simple question, react, share a short pattern you're seeing, then ask for a quick meeting. Don't stack five discovery questions.

•
Sound like you're calling a coworker

Short sentences. Normal words. Calm pace. If a line is hard to say out loud, rewrite it until it sounds like you.

Curious method

Best for: most live connects

Start with a low-pressure question that gets them talking before you explain anything.

"When it comes to company devices, what's usually the biggest headache for your team?"

Pattern method

Best for: creating a reason to meet

After they answer, mention a common pattern that may or may not apply.

"A lot of teams already have a tool, but still hit gaps around a clearer view, mobile security, or manual support work."

Referral method

Best for: unclear title

Ask for direction instead of forcing the pitch on the wrong person.

"You may not be the right person, but who usually owns device management on your side?"
